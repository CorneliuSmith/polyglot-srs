# Curriculum reference — Hebrew (he)

The app's grammar path in teaching order. These point titles are exactly what the learner sees on their cards, and weak grammar items arrive labeled with them — use the same names when coaching, and sequence new material in this order.

## A1
- Personal pronouns and grammatical gender — Choose the right pronoun for gender and number
- No verb "to be" in the present tense — Build a sentence with no linking verb
- Adjective gender agreement — Match an adjective's ending to its noun's gender
- The definite article ה־ — Say "the" in Hebrew
- Present-tense verbs change with gender and number — Conjugate a present-tense verb for its subject
- Negation with לא — Say what isn't or doesn't happen
- Existence: יש and אין — Say that something does or doesn't exist
- Possession with של — Say who something belongs to
- Infinitives after רוצה and אפשר — Chain a second verb onto the first without conjugating it
- Yes/no questions and question words — Ask a question in Hebrew

## A2
- Past tense pa'al: the singular persons — Put a basic verb into the past for one person
- Past tense pa'al: the plural persons — Put a basic verb into the past for several people
- Future tense pa'al — Say what will happen
- Prepositions with pronoun endings — Say to me, to him, at my place
- The object marker את — Mark a definite direct object
- Numbers and gender agreement — Count nouns with the right form of the number
- Possession with יש ל־ — Say what someone has
- Noun and adjective plural agreement — Make nouns plural and keep the adjective in step
- Present tense pi'el — Use the second verb pattern in the present
- Present tense hif'il — Use the causative verb pattern in the present

## B1
- Roots and binyanim: how the system works — Read a verb as a root plus a pattern
- Pi'el in the past and the future — Conjugate a pi'el verb outside the present
- Hif'il in the past and the future — Conjugate a hif'il verb outside the present
- Hitpa'el: reflexive and reciprocal verbs — Say what someone does to themselves or to each other
- The construct state (smichut) — Join two nouns without של
- Verbal nouns (shem pe'ula) — Name the activity a verb describes
- Real conditionals with אם — State a condition that may well come true
- Relative clauses with ש־ — Attach a clause to a noun
- Comparatives with יותר and הכי — Compare two things and pick the top one

## B2
- Nif'al: the middle and passive pattern — Use the pattern for things that happen to the subject
- Pu'al and huf'al: recognizing the passives — Read the two patterns that exist only as passives
- Unreal conditionals with אילו and לו — Talk about what would happen if things were different
- Temporal clauses: כש־, לפני ש־, אחרי ש־ — Anchor one event in time relative to another
- Indirect speech with ש־ — Report what someone said
- Object pronouns and their literary suffixed forms — Say me, him, them as the object of a verb

## C1
- Formal and colloquial register — Choose the connector that fits the situation
- אשר beside everyday ש־ — Use the written-register relative word
- Long construct chains — Read and build a chain of three or more nouns

## C2
- The vav-conversive in quotation and idiom — Recognize the biblical tense system where it survives
- Idiomatic shifts between binyanim — Handle roots whose meaning jumps unpredictably between patterns
- Switching register inside one text — Move between written and spoken Hebrew as the text demands
