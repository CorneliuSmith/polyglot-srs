# Common learner errors — Hebrew

- **Gender agreement dropped partway through a sentence.** A learner
  correctly genders the subject pronoun but leaves the verb or adjective
  in the default masculine form (היא הולך instead of היא הולכת). Point at
  the specific word that still needs to agree, not just the sentence as a
  whole.
- **Looking for a verb "to be" in the present tense.** English reflexes
  make learners insert one, or hunt for it when reading. Remind them
  Hebrew present-tense sentences link subject and predicate directly —
  there is nothing missing.
- **את/אתה vs. אֵת confusion.** The feminine "you" (את) and the definite
  direct-object marker (אֶת, same spelling without niqqud) look identical
  in unvocalized text — context (a pronoun slot vs. before a definite
  object) is the only way to tell them apart, and beginners default to
  reading every את as "you."
- **Root-pattern overgeneralization.** Once a learner sees one verb built
  from a root in one binyan, they assume every root works in every binyan
  with a predictable meaning shift — some roots simply don't exist in a
  given pattern, or shift meaning unpredictably.
- **Definite article stacking errors.** Forgetting that ה־ attaches
  directly with no separate word, or over-applying it to already-definite
  proper nouns.
- **Root vs. surface-letter confusion.** Weak roots (containing א, ה, ו, or
  י) don't show all three consonants on the surface, so learners
  misidentify the root and can't predict related forms — walk through the
  weak letter's typical disappearance/transformation explicitly.
