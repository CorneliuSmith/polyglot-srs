# Curriculum reference — Tagalog (tl)

The app's grammar path in teaching order. These point titles are exactly what the learner sees on their cards, and weak grammar items arrive labeled with them — use the same names when coaching, and sequence new material in this order.

## A1
- The ang phrase marks the sentence's topic — Identify what the sentence is 'about'
- ng marks a non-topic actor, object, or possessor — Mark a noun that isn't the topic
- sa marks location, direction, or a specific place — Say where something happens
- Actor-focus verbs with the -um- infix — Build actor-focus verbs from a root
- The linker na — Connect a describing word to what it describes
- mga marks the plural — Make a noun plural
- Existence and possession: may and wala — Say that something exists, or that you have it
- hindi negates verbs and adjectives — Say what isn't true or didn't happen

## A2
- mag- actor-focus verbs, beside -um- — Use the other actor-focus prefix, and hear the difference
- Aspect: completed, incompleted, contemplated — Mark when the action stands, not when it happened
- Object focus with -in — Make the thing acted on the topic
- Locative focus with -an — Make the place or recipient the topic
- ang pronouns — Use the pronouns that take the topic slot
- ng pronouns — Say who did it when the object is the topic
- sa pronouns and possession — Say to me, for you, and mine
- si and sina: names take their own marker — Mark people's names correctly
- Questions: ba and the question words — Ask yes/no and wh- questions
- ma- adjectives and the linker — Describe things and attach the description

## B1
- i- focus: benefactive and instrument — Make the thing given, or the tool, the topic
- ma- and maka-: ability and the involuntary — Say could, managed to, and happened to
- Enclitic particles and their order — Place na, pa, ba, po and daw correctly
- ay inversion — Write the formal, subject-first order
- Comparison: mas, kaysa, pinaka- — Say more, than, and most
- Pseudo-verbs: gusto, ayaw, dapat, puwede — Say want, don't want, should, may
- may and mayroon — Say there is, and have
- Counting: native and Spanish numbers — Use the right number system for the job
- Deictics: ito, iyan, iyon — Point at things by distance from the speaker

## B2
- Causatives: magpa- and pa-...-in — Say you had someone do something
- Recent perfective: ka- + reduplication — Say something has only just happened
- Nominalisation: pag-, pagka-, ka-...-an — Turn actions and qualities into nouns
- Relative clauses with na and -ng — Attach a whole clause to a noun
- Conditionals: kung, kapag, sana — Say if, when, and if only
- Reciprocal and social affixes — Say to each other, and to go along with
- pang- instruments and other noun prefixes — Name the tool and the season

## C1
- Register: Filipino, deep Tagalog, and Taglish — Move between the three registers educated speakers use
- Focus choice as information structure — Pick the focus that puts the right thing in view
- Aspect subtleties: na, pa, and the contemplated — Say already, still, and not-yet precisely

## C2
- Literary and formal Filipino — Read the register of literature and official texts
- Lexicalised affixation — Recognise affixed words that have left their roots behind
- Code-switching as a system — Switch languages mid-sentence the way bilinguals do
