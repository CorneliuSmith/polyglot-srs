# Common learner errors — Tagalog

- **ang used for the English "subject" instead of the focus-marked topic.**
  Learners import the English notion of subject and mark the actor with
  ang even when the verb's affix has focused the object or location
  instead. Ask "which affix is on the verb?" before choosing the marker.
- **Wrong focus affix chosen for what the sentence is about.** Learners
  default to actor-focus (-um-/mag-) even when the sentence is really
  about the object or place, producing a grammatical but focus-mismatched
  sentence. Show the same event with a different focus to make the
  contrast concrete.
- **ng and mga confused.** Both can precede a noun, but ng marks a
  non-topic actor/object/possessor while mga marks plurality — they do
  completely different jobs and can even co-occur (ng mga bata).
- **na/-ng linker dropped between a modifier and its noun.** English
  doesn't need a linker (a good child), so learners often skip na entirely
  (bata mabuti instead of bata na mabuti / batang mabuti).
- **Aspect read as tense.** Learners map completed/incomplete/contemplated
  aspect directly onto English past/present/future and get confused when
  an incomplete-aspect verb describes something that already started.
- **Pronoun clitics placed at the end of the sentence.** English puts
  pronouns wherever the noun would go; Tagalog clusters short pronouns
  (ko, mo, niya…) right after the first word of the predicate, not at the
  end.
