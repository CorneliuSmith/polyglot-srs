# Common learner errors — French (fr)

_Extracted from source documents; review before folding into ERRORS.md._

## Present tense of s'appeler (saying your name)
- Forgetting the reflexive pronoun (saying 'je appelle' instead of 'je m'appelle').
- Forgetting the elision: it is 'je m'appelle', not 'je me appelle'.
- Writing double l with nous/vous: it is nous appelons, vous appelez.

## Present tense of habiter (regular -er verb)
- Pronouncing the initial h of habiter — it is completely silent.
- Writing 'je habite' instead of 'j'habite'.
- Pronouncing the -ent ending of ils habitent; it is silent.

## Present tense of être
- Adding an article after être with a profession (saying 'je suis un professeur').
- Confusing tu es with il est in writing.

## The mute h (h muet)
- Pronouncing the h as in English ('house') — in French it is silent.
- Blocking elision after a mute h: write j'habite, l'hiver, not je habite, le hiver.

## Saying where you live: à with cities, en / aux with countries
- Using à before a country name (saying 'j'habite à France').
- Using en with a plural country: it must be aux Etats-Unis.
- Forgetting that country names have gender, which decides the preposition.

## No indefinite article with professions
- Adding un/une before the profession: 'je suis un ingénieur' is wrong.
- Translating 'a' from English word for word after être.

## 'ils' or 'elles'? Choosing the pronoun for 'they'
- Using elles for a mixed group — one male makes the whole group ils.
- Forgetting that both pronouns take the same verb form (ils sont / elles sont).
