# Curriculum reference — Indonesian (id)

The app's grammar path in teaching order. These point titles are exactly what the learner sees on their cards, and weak grammar items arrive labeled with them — use the same names when coaching, and sequence new material in this order.

## A1
- Verbs don't conjugate — Use one verb form for every subject and tense
- Adjectives follow the noun — Put adjectives in the right place
- Negation: tidak vs. bukan — Pick the right word for "not"
- Possession by word order — Show ownership with no possessive marker
- Plural by reduplication — Make a noun plural by doubling it
- Time markers before the verb: sudah, belum, akan — Show past, not-yet, and future without changing the verb
- Yes/no questions with apakah — Ask a yes/no question without reordering the sentence
- Formal vs. informal "you"; inclusive vs. exclusive "we" — Choose the pronoun that matches the social situation

## A2
- The meN- prefix: active transitive verbs — Form active verbs and hear the nasal change
- The ber- prefix: intransitive and stative verbs — Say what someone does or has, with no object
- The di- passive: putting the object first — Lead with what was affected, not who did it
- Comparison: lebih, daripada, paling, ter- — Say more, less, and most
- Classifiers: orang, ekor, buah — Count things the way Indonesian counts them
- ada: existence, having, and 'there is' — Say something exists or is present
- Question words — Ask what, who, where, when, why, how
- Modality: bisa, boleh, harus, mau — Say can, may, must, want
- Place prepositions: di, ke, dari — Say where something is, where it's going, where it came from
- Aspect markers: sedang, masih, pernah, baru — Say in-progress, still, ever, and just-now

## B1
- The -kan suffix: causative and benefactive — Make someone do it, or do it for someone
- The -i suffix: location and repetition — Point the verb at a place, or at repeated action
- peN- nouns: the doer — Name the person or thing that does it
- ke-...-an and per-...-an nouns — Build abstract nouns from anything
- yang: the all-purpose relativiser — Join a description to a noun
- Passive type 1 and type 2 — Choose the right passive for the person doing it
- Purpose: untuk, supaya, agar — Say what something is for
- Conditionals: kalau, jika, bila — Say if
- The se- prefix: one, same, whole — Say same-as, one, and all-of

## B2
- The ter- prefix: accidental, stative, capable — Say it happened by itself, or by accident
- memper-: causatives on adjectives — Make something more X
- Reduplication beyond the plural — Use doubling for casualness, variety, and reciprocity
- Reported speech with bahwa — Report what someone said or thought
- Discourse particles: -lah, -kah, -pun — Add emphasis, question force, and 'even'
- Formal connectors — Write the way a newspaper writes
- Agentless passives and oleh — Leave the doer out, or name it

## C1
- Register: baku, colloquial, and Jakarta speech — Move between the written standard and how people talk
- Nominalisation in formal prose — Read the noun-heavy style of official Indonesian
- Affix pairs that shift meaning — Tell apart verbs that share a root

## C2
- Switching register inside one text — Change level mid-text the way Indonesians do
- Lexicalised affixation — Recognise affixed words that no longer mean what the parts mean
- Journalistic style: fronting and ellipsis — Read headlines and dense reporting
