# Common learner errors — Indonesian

- **tidak/bukan mixed up.** Learners default to tidak for everything,
  since it's usually taught first, and forget bukan is required to negate
  a noun or an identity statement (Ini bukan buku, not *Ini tidak buku).
- **meN- nasal assimilation skipped or wrong.** Learners attach meN-
  unchanged instead of assimilating it to the root's first sound
  (meN- + pukul should become memukul, not "menpukul"). Drill the
  assimilation rule by consonant class explicitly.
- **Adjective or possessor placed before the noun.** English word order
  leaks in — rumah besar becomes "besar rumah," rumah saya becomes "saya
  rumah." Rebuild by asking "which word is the THING?" first.
- **Verb conjugated for tense or person.** Learners instinctively try to
  add an ending for "he/she" or "past," forgetting Indonesian verbs never
  change form — remind them the marker word (sudah/akan/sedang) carries
  that information instead.
- **Reduplication used redundantly with banyak ("many").** Since both mark
  plurality, learners sometimes stack them (banyak buku-buku) where native
  usage picks one or the other.
- **di- passive avoided in favor of an awkward meN- active.** English
  speakers under-use the di- passive because English marks passive
  voice as marked/unusual; in Indonesian, di- is the everyday default
  whenever the actor isn't the point of the sentence.
