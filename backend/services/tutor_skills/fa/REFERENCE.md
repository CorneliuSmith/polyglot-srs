# Curriculum reference — Persian (fa)

The app's grammar path in teaching order. These point titles are exactly what the learner sees on their cards, and weak grammar items arrive labeled with them — use the same names when coaching, and sequence new material in this order.

## A1
- Personal pronouns and budan ("to be") — Say who/what something is
- Verb-final word order (SOV) — Build a basic sentence in Persian order
- Personal verb endings: -am, -i, -ad — Conjugate a present-tense verb for its subject
- Negation with na- / nemi- — Say what you don't do or don't want
- Adjectives follow the noun — Put adjectives in the right place
- Yes/no questions: same word order, just a question mark — Ask a yes/no question without reordering the sentence
- The plural suffix -ha — Make a noun plural
- Ra marks a definite direct object — Mark a direct object you already know about
- Possession with ezafe: ketab-e man — Say whose something is
- Possessive endings: -am, -at, -ash — Attach the owner straight onto the noun
- The verb dashtan ("to have") — Say what you have

## A2
- Simple past: the past stem — Say what happened
- Two verb stems: present and past — Pick the right stem for the tense
- Past negation with na- — Say what didn't happen
- Present perfect: rafte-am — Report something already done
- Comparatives and superlatives: -tar, -tarin — Compare two things, or pick the top one
- Object pronoun clitics: didamash — Recognize a pronoun object stuck on the verb
- Question words in a full sentence — Ask what, where, when and why
- Habitual past: miraftam — Say what you used to do
- Light-verb compounds: kar kardan, harf zadan — Use the compound verbs Persian builds everything from
- Numbers and the counter ta — Count things the Persian way

## B1
- The subjunctive: beravam — Say what you want, must or might do
- Future with khaham raft — Write about the future
- Conditionals type 1: agar + subjunctive — Talk about a real possibility
- Relative clauses with ke — Add a which/who clause to a noun
- Indirect speech with ke — Report what someone said
- Ezafe chains: dar-e khane-ye dustam — Stack several linked nouns in a row
- Infinitives as nouns: raftan, khandan — Use a verb as the subject or object of a sentence
- tavanestan: saying what you can do — Say what is and isn't possible

## B2
- Past perfect: rafte budam — Set one past event before another
- Unreal conditionals: agar miraftam — Talk about what would have been
- Passive with shodan: neveshte shod — Say what was done, without saying who did it
- Causatives in -andan (recognition) — Recognize "make someone do it" verbs
- ta: purpose and until — Join two clauses with "so that" or "until"
- Written vs. colloquial Persian (recognition) — Tell the written standard from the spoken form

## C1
- Formal and administrative register — Read and write official Persian
- Arabic broken plurals in Persian (recognition) — Recognize the Arabic plurals used in formal Persian
- Literary past forms: baraft, hami (recognition) — Read the past tense of older Persian prose

## C2
- Classical verse conventions (recognition) — Read Persian poetry on its own terms
- Idioms and light-verb nuance — Say it the way Persian says it
- Register shifting: deference and distance — Move between polite, neutral and intimate Persian
