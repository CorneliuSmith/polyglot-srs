# Curriculum reference — Latin (la)

The app's grammar path in teaching order. These point titles are exactly what the learner sees on their cards, and weak grammar items arrive labeled with them — use the same names when coaching, and sequence new material in this order.

## A1
- Nominative and accusative: subject and direct object — Tell who does the action from who or what receives it
- First declension nouns: -a / -ae feminine — Recognize and form the most common noun pattern
- Second declension nouns: -us masculine and -um neuter — Learn the other core noun pattern
- sum, esse — the irregular verb "to be" — Say what something or someone IS
- First conjugation present tense (-are verbs) — Conjugate the largest family of Latin verbs
- Adjective-noun agreement — Match adjective endings to the noun they describe
- Prepositions: in + accusative vs. in + ablative — Choose the right case after "in"
- Word order: Latin puts case marking to work — Understand why word order is flexible in Latin
- Second conjugation present tense (-ere verbs with -e-) — Conjugate the video/habeo family in the present
- possum, posse — "to be able" with an infinitive — Say what someone can or cannot do

## A2
- Imperfect tense (-bam): what used to happen — Describe ongoing or repeated action in the past
- Future tense (-bo/-bis and -am/-es) — Say what will happen
- Third declension nouns: rex, regis and urbs, urbis — Handle the biggest and most varied noun family
- Third declension neuters: corpus, tempus, nomen — Spot the neuters whose subject and object forms match
- Personal pronouns: ego, tu, nos, vos — Use I/you/we pronouns for emphasis and as objects
- Demonstratives: hic "this" and ille "that" — Point at things near and far
- Third and fourth conjugation present (dico, audio) — Complete the present tense across all four conjugations
- Imperatives: giving commands — Tell someone to do something
- Questions: -ne, quis and quid — Ask yes/no and who/what questions
- Genitive and dative at work — Say whose something is and to whom it goes

## B1
- Perfect tense: amavi, vidi, dixi — Report completed past actions
- Pluperfect tense: amaveram "I had loved" — Reach further back — past before the past
- Fourth and fifth declension: manus and dies — Finish the noun system with the -us and -es families
- Comparison of adjectives: altior, altissimus — Say taller, tallest, very tall
- Adverbs: bene, fortiter, celeriter — Describe how an action is done
- Present passive: amatur "is loved" — Flip the sentence — the subject receives the action
- Perfect passive participle and perfect passive — Say what was done with participle + est
- Relative clauses: qui, quae, quod — Attach a whole clause to a noun

## B2
- The subjunctive mood: present and imperfect forms — Form the mood of wish, doubt, and dependent clauses
- Purpose clauses with ut and ne — Say why someone acts — in order to
- Result clauses: tam ... ut — Say that something is SO ... that ...
- Cum-clauses: when, since, although — Read the three faces of cum + subjunctive
- Indirect statement: accusative + infinitive — Report what someone says, knows, or thinks
- Ablative absolute — Fold background events into two ablative words
- Deponent verbs: loquor, sequor — Passive endings, active meaning

## C1
- Gerund, gerundive, and the passive periphrastic — Handle Latin's verbal noun and the 'must be done' construction
- Indirect questions and the sequence of tenses — Fold a question into another sentence
- Conditionals: the three types of si-clause — Distinguish real, should-would, and contrary-to-fact conditions

## C2
- Oratio obliqua: sustained indirect discourse — Read whole reported speeches the way Caesar wrote them
- Poetic word order: reading real classical lines — Untangle hyperbaton in verses every Roman knew
- Register: Ciceronian period and colloquial Latin — Hear the difference between the speech and the letter
