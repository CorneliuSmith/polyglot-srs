## Twenty refusals, and what they were

The largest class this time is **a rule the writer invented that the point's own drills falsify** — seven of the twenty. A Turkish causative rule saying the person made to act takes the dative, with drill 5 as the counter-example. A Swahili claim that the singular imperative is 'the exception', when the plural is irregular too. A Hindi presumptive said to agree with the subject exactly as the future does, for a shape this very point teaches otherwise. An Arabic rule that every first- and second-person past suffix begins with ت, contradicted by the table printed directly above it.

The second class is **information dropped without saying so** — six. Arabic paradigm examples lost their English gloss; a Hindi fraction lost its time use (पौना is also 'quarter to'); a Hindi honorific note lost the line that over-respecting a peer can sound sarcastic.

## What landed

Among the 539 corrections, the recurring shape is still a paradigm that stops before the forms the point drills:

- **Arabic personal pronouns** listed no plural `أنتم` at all, while the point drills it twice.
- **Arabic attached pronouns** stopped at `ها`, so `نا` (our) — drilled twice, in the point's own paradigm — appeared nowhere.
- **Arabic adjective agreement** said the adjective copies gender and definiteness. The hardest drill in the point tests the accusative on both words, and case was not in the list.
- **Arabic negation** presented two negators as covering the language; `لم` and `لن` have their own points in the same course.
