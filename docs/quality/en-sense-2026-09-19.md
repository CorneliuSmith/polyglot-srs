# The English middle band, judged row by row (19 Sep 2026)

The 18 Sep measurement sampled 358 English rows and projected ~853 carrying a
sense a learner does not meet, concentrated in ranks 2,001–6,000. Its first
recommendation was to repair that band, because the English definition is the
upstream cause of the Arabic gloss the beta reviewer complained about and of
the same defect in all nine support locales.

**This is that band, not sampled but judged in full.**

## 1. What was done

| | |
|---|---:|
| rows in ranks 2,001–6,000 | 3,982 |
| already carrying a gloss override (left alone) | 56 |
| **judged** | **3,926** |
| maker agents (40 rows each, `content_judge`'s `SENSE_RULES`) | 99 |
| checker agents (20 findings each, asked to refute) | 26 |

In-session maker–checker through the Workflow tool. **No API key was spent**
(owner directive, quality rule 26).

## 2. What it found

| verdict | n | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| primary — the sense a learner meets | 2,710 | 69.0% |
| secondary — a real sense, not the one the rank was earned by (not a finding) | 410 | 10.4% |
| **rare** — genuine but technical, dated, slang or regional | **289** | **7.4%** |
| **wrong** — not a usable sense at all | **223** | **5.7%** |
| unsure — routed to a human, never applied | 294 | 7.5% |

**512 findings, 13.0%**, split almost evenly between the two halves of the band
(249 in 2,001–4,000, 263 in 4,001–6,000). The 18 Sep sample put those halves at
15.0% and 16.9% from 60 rows each; a full census inside a wide interval is the
expected outcome, and 13.0% is now the number.

By category: `not_a_sense` 219, `technical` 165, `dated` 93, `slang` 21,
`regional` 10, `wrong_pos` 3, `relation_only` 1.

## 3. What the checker refused

The checker re-judged all 512 with the same charter and a different question —
*is the existing definition really unusable, and is the proposed replacement
correct* — and wrote its own replacement whenever it would word one better.

- **60 (11.7%) it refused outright**: the maker had judged the wording rather
  than the sense, or the word genuinely is technical at that rank.
- **86 more** it agreed with below the 0.7 confidence floor either side.
- **366 confirmed**, of which **236 shipped** after the mechanical gates.

That 11.7% is the maker's false-alarm rate on this question, measured. It is
the reason the checker exists and the reason nothing ships on one opinion.

Worked examples of the 236:

- **`despite`** (2,287) — "lack of respect accompanied by a feeling of intense
  dislike" → "in spite of; without being stopped or affected by something".
  The card was teaching a noun nobody uses for the commonest preposition.
- **`galaxy`** (3,123) — "a splendid assemblage (especially of famous people)"
  → the astronomical sense.
- **`auction`** (4,094) — "a variety of bridge … now generally superseded by
  contract bridge" → "a public sale where goods are sold to the person who
  offers the most money".
- **`fang`** (5,498) — "a Bantu language spoken in Cameroon" → "a long, sharp
  tooth, as of a dog or snake".
- **`terry`** (2,376) — "English actress (1847–1928)" → terrycloth.
- **`battery`** (2,614) — "group of guns or missile launchers operated together
  at one place" → "a device that stores and supplies electric power".

Every applied replacement passed five mechanical gates before it was written
(`scripts/apply_en_sense_fixes.py`): not circular, not relation-only, not a
name description, not noun-shaped on a verb row, and not meta-commentary.

**The last gate was written because the pass needed it.** Asked for a
definition, three judges wrote a verdict *about the row* into the field a
learner reads — "not an English word — a corpus artefact with no sense to
define; the card should be retired" was going to ship as the definition of
`chffffff`. Each is a correct observation and a catastrophic definition. The
rule generalises past this pass: **any field a model fills needs a predicate
for "the model answered a different question."**

One more gate was wrong and had to be loosened: the name predicate matched
"**the name** given to a book, film, song, or other work" — the correct
definition of `title` — and routed a real repair into the retirement bucket.
One false positive in 120, found by re-reading the bucket rather than the code
(quality rule 19: verify every hit).

## 5. Two classes this pass found and deliberately did not fix

### 119 personal names, and 1,231 headwords the course never taught

A third of the confirmed findings are personal names — `greg`, `maggie`,
`joey`, `julia`, `taylor` — where the honest replacement is "a male given
name", which is the definition the owner's **25 Aug rule** retires: nobody can
produce `Greg` from it. The rule is settled and its criterion is objective, but
it was applied once, to a table the owner read. This pass counts the class and
stops there.

Measuring it properly turned up the bigger number. English definitions come
from WordNet at seed time for any row the file leaves blank
(`seed_english.py`: "WordNet still resolves anything the file leaves blank"),
so a blank column is not a blank card — **except when WordNet has nothing
either**. Resolved through the seeder's own `best_synset` for every blank row:

| | n |
|---|---:|
| English rows with a blank committed definition | 1,231 |
| ...that WordNet also cannot fill, so the seeder skips the row | **1,231** |
| ...of those, present in production at all | **0** (of 9,062 English rows) |
| ...of those, tokenizer shrapnel (`comin`, `thinkin`, `argh`, 1–3 letters) | 231 |
| ...a real headword WordNet does not carry (mostly given names) | 1,000 |
| ...in the top 2,000 | **0** |

**1,231 headwords are missing from the English course**, about an eighth of
its frequency list. (This page first called them cards with no definition;
checked against production afterwards, none of them is a card — `seed_english`
skips a row it cannot define, so the word never reaches `vocabulary`. A missing
word is a worse defect than a thin definition and a cheaper repair, since the
fix adds cards rather than deleting them. CHECKS §42 carries the correction.)
The class was already known — `seed_english` logs it and DEBT.md has carried "1,389 headwords, 125
fixed, 1,264 deferred" since that logging went in — so this is a
re-measurement of a recorded backlog, not a discovery. What is new is that the
audit produces the number now, rather than a seeder warning nobody reads. It is entirely a tail phenomenon, which is why every pass through
the top bands missed it. The treatment splits: the shrapnel is a retirement,
the names are the 25 Aug rule, and the residue — `amongst`, `beside`, `toward`,
`versus`, `whereas`, `theirs`, `thee` — is a real gap WordNet structurally
cannot fill, because it does not define function words.

**This needs the owner**, because it is ~1,200 retirements against a rule that
was applied once with a table in front of them, not a mechanical repair.

### The 32 that were judged on an empty field

32 of the 236 applied rows had a blank committed definition, so the judge was
shown nothing and its finding was "there is nothing here". That is the same
shape as the mistake this programme made in September — judging a corpus that
is not the one production serves — so each was resolved through
`best_synset` before it shipped. **All 32 get nothing from WordNet either**, so
the card really is empty and a definition is a strict improvement. They are the
most valuable rows in the set and they are called out here because the
reasoning, not the result, is what makes them safe.

## 6. What this does not say

- **Not human review.** Maker–checker with a mechanical gate, and the per-row
  reasoning from both sides is in `data/eval/en_sense_2026-09-19.jsonl` — 512
  rows, each with the maker's verdict and the checker's argument. A reviewer
  who wants to audit it has the whole trail (quality rule 30).
- **Only the middle band.** Ranks 1–2,000 were repaired by Phase 2d and 6,001+
  is largely technical vocabulary where the rare-looking sense genuinely is the
  word. Neither was judged here.
- **The support locales are not fixed by this.** Repairing the English
  definition removes the *upstream* cause of a wrong Arabic or Turkish gloss;
  it does not rewrite the gloss. The 24.6% Arabic divergence measured on 18 Sep
  is still there, and a re-run after a re-seed is what would show movement.
- **294 unsure rows were not touched.**
