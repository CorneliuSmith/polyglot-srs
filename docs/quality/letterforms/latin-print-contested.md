# Latin print: the eight letters the models disagree about

Run 3 of `gemini-brief-lifted-print.md` asked two things: anything left
out of runs 1 and 2 (nothing was — `unknown: []`), and a reading of which
letters in this alphabet are most contested between teaching models.
This is that reading, kept because the Latin print table now rests on a
**single** curriculum (Handwriting Without Tears) and this names the
places where a second source would most likely disagree with it.

Nothing reads this file. It is for whoever next wonders why a letter
looks the way it does, and for choosing what to re-source first if the
owner's eye lands on one of these.

| letter | what the models disagree about |
|---|---|
| `a` | Continuous retraced "magic c" base (Zaner-Bloser, D'Nealian) vs. lifted ball-and-stick (HWT, traditional UK). |
| `d` | Continuous retraced bowl first, to prevent reversal, vs. stem-first lifted form. |
| `f` | Continuous hook and stem vs. separate top hook and straight stem. |
| `g` | Simple open hook tail vs. closed loop tail. |
| `k` | Two-stroke continuous chevron for the right side vs. three distinct strokes. |
| `q` | Straight descender vs. descender hooked right vs. hooked left. |
| `t` | Straight vertical stem (US models) vs. curved baseline hook to the right (UK/Australian/NZ models). |
| `M` | Vertical outer stems vs. splayed ones; centre vertex on the baseline vs. floating at the midline. |

Three of these are already live decisions rather than open questions:

- **`d`** is settled by the owner, not by a source: stem then bowl. The
  table records the sources' bowl-first order in `disagreement`.
- **`a`** is settled by the same reasoning that produced this whole
  table — the retrace is a lift, so the lifted ball-and-stick form wins.
- **`k`** is the one letter the source change cost us. HWT teaches two
  strokes, Zaner-Bloser three, our walk draws three, so `k` now
  disagrees with its table where it used to agree. If a second source is
  ever added, this is the letter to look at first.

The remaining five are open, and `t` is the one most likely to matter:
the app teaches British-English courses among others, and the UK model's
curved baseline hook is a different letter from the US straight stem,
not a different way of drawing the same one.
